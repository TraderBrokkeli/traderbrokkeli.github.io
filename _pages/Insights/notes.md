# [Interivew with Brando on Words of Rizdom](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AfPPTnJAn04)

- Sizing for zero means you're mentally prepared for it to go to 0.
- Risk more based on the quality of the setup (need some records to prove this).
- You *should* (according to Brando) make more money holding long runs risking less than risking more and taking profit early because when you've too much at stake, you will always sell early.
- You can't control the outcome but you can control how much you are willing to lose.
- Focus on the fundamentals (sizing to zero, focusing on when to trade etc.) are skills that will help you when you want to scale to a larger account.
- Don't be attached to money, it's only used to make more money, help your friends, family etc.
- You have to be ok with losing if you want to get to the top.
- When you're starting out, you have to be very careful with what you trade with because your margin of error is very small (not much capital to work with).
- Trading is a lot about taking 1 step forward 2 steps back so sometimes it can feel very frustrating with the lack of progress but you're learning a lot about yourself like your trading style, your risk appetite, how you handle losses etc.
- There's nothing wrong with failing but as long as you see what you did wrong and you pivot. The ability to pivot is important in trading.
- The feedback cycle in options is much quicker than in like traditional brick & mortar so you should take advantage of this and adjust quickly based on your failures.
- There's a time of day where you perform best. You need to find that window.
- There's a trade setup that you perform the best at. You need to find that setup. For Brando, that's breakout trades.
- Waiting for support resistance to hit and waiting for price to react there then you take a trade.
- The waiting part is the hardest part.
- Alignment of index with sector leading stock produces the best gains for Brando (relative strength).
- You also have to KIV what the other stocks (esp. the mega caps) are doing because they also move the markets.
- The best traders use simple strategies.
- You don't have to be a genius to find the right trades, you just have to be patient and waiting for the right levels to trade off from.
- Indicators can work but when you're in the wrong trade, you'll find 10 diff reasons for it to work and if you're doing that, it's a recipe for failure. You'll find reasons not to sell because "it's still bullish in a higher timeframe".
- You should only have 1-2 reasons to be in a trade (level, overall sentiment / catalyst).
- Brando doesnt read fundamentals / financial reports, just wait for certain levels and have the conviction to take the trade.
- Sizing for zero also forces you to only take the best trades that you're willing for it to go to zero.
- Every trader will sometimes break their rules (usually cause of ego).
- After a huge loss, size down dramatically to build up confidence again.
- Pro traders are always on their toes everyday. They don't just click a few buttons and make a few millions like that. There's always a lot to learn about yourself and the market. Complacency is real esp. when you've had some success.
- Having a secondary source of income is not a bad idea since it takes the pressure of you.
- You don't always need to make money every month. Some months will be better than others, you just need to find these pockets of momentum.
- Don't immediately change strategies just because it stopped working for a few months.
- Most of your profit will come from a handful of trades.
- Focus on a couple of stocks and really understand them.
- By recording your trades, and assuming you apply the same strategy, you can see when your strategy starts to get less and less effective. That's when you should start scaling down until it becomes effective again. The goal is to recognize when your strategy is not working in the current environment.
- You need to study your losing trades so that you know what your mistakes are really costing you. Averaging down is a common big one.
- Knowing when you should be trading is the most important e.g. if you cannot handle the volatility of the first 1-2 hours then avoid it.
- Do a quick review everyday after your trading is over to see if you followed your rules, are you trading outside your "window", are you going down a new rabbithole of bad habits etc.
- It's easy to trade just to see action.
- Don't scale up just because you can because maybe you won't be able to stomach that loss.
- Brando only takes A+ setups because what's the point of entering on a c setup? Having said that, you should still have a way of scaling risk. Not all trades are the same e.g. you shouldn't risk the same for 0dte vs weeklies even though both are a+ setups.
- Primarily focuses on weeklies (buys on mon, expires on fri)
- Brando only has like a maximum of 3-4 trades opened at any one time.
- You keep rolling the trade until it doesn't work anymore.
- You shouldn't notice yourself scaling up i.e. it shouldn't be once you hit the 7 fig mark, suddenly you 3x your risk.
- To truly enjoy the process, you have to like this world of volatility, stocks and the unknown (risk).
- When you start with a small amt of money, you can't take a trade everyday. You won't be able to make X amt everyday. You have to wait for the right opportunity e.g. when certain levels break then you buy a long dated option.
- You have be able to adapt quickly and not be stubborn. The market doesn't care what you want. You may have taken X setup and it failed but just because it presents itself again doesn't mean you should take it again and again just to make it back. When it fails at an important level, you need to let it drop.

- Having the index line up with the stock is everything.

# [Vincent Desiano's trading strategy 2026 ed.](https://youtu.be/jGIef5CCsxU)

- If you think you are special, think again. You have to put in the work like everyone else. You cannot skip the education / study phase.
- Levels are just the location. Price tells you the reaction. Levels without price is useless.
- daily SMA is not the same as SMA. SMA changes based on the interval you're using on the chart. Daily SMA sticks to daily regardless of your interval.
- Break and retest is Desi's bread n butter. 
- If it matches your setup, you must take the trade. Do not let fear and hesistation hold you back.
- 10/20/2 rule. Risk 10% per trade. Your stop must be less than 20% of how much you risk i.e. you're risking 2% per trade and at minimum 2 contracts so you can scale out otw up.
- Scaling out helps a lot with psychology (decreasing emotions and fear).

## How to break and retest

- Always align the stock you are trading with ES/NQ. If ES/NQ is running into a resistance, you wouldn't want to be taking the long on the stock.
- You dont want to clutter your chart with too many lines. Just the key levels where a strong reaction / multiple reactions occurred at.
- If price is anywhere in between the levels, you do not trade.
- You cannot enter on the break candle. Once a level has been broken, you have to wait for it to retest the key level.
- At the key level, you must decide, based on the experience and how price is moving, whether or not to take the trade. You will never have complete information.
- You can choose to wait for the confirmation candle to close bullish (engulfing, hammer).
- However, the longer you wait to enter (for more confirmation), the larger your risk becomes because your SL must always be at the retest level. You need to accept that there will always be risk involved with trading (read trading in the zone by )
- Your first TP (where you scale out some) is the high that accompanied the breakout prior to the retest. Your stop is at the breakout level.
